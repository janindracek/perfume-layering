# Perfume layering — a course in seventeen wearing days

A walkthrough course for learning to smell, and then to layer. Written for someone who
owns some perfume, likes it, and cannot yet say why one combination holds and another
turns to mud.

**Live version: https://janindracek.github.io/perfume-layering/**

## The claim it is built on

Bottles differ in how much they assert. Some say everything they have to say alone; others
leave room — the ones your own notes call quiet, subtle, powdery, unremarkable. Layering is
the question of what goes into the room the quiet ones leave, which makes them the material
rather than the leftovers. The axis is how much space a scent occupies, not how good it is:
a perfume can be rated excellent *and* be open, and for layering the openness is the
operative property.

## How the lessons work

Each lesson is one wearing day, and runs on a clock rather than a description:

- **A table of what to read and when.** What is evaporating at 0:30, 2:00 and 6:00, and
  what to write at each. Readings taken at random times are not comparable to each other.
- **Every step carries its mechanism and its failure mode.** A *why* box saying what is
  physically happening, and a *watch for* box naming the specific mistake available at that
  moment — not generic caution.
- **A prediction that locks before the result opens.** You commit to what the second
  perfume will do to the first, then find out. Being wrong in a recorded way is the point;
  it is the only part of the day that produces knowledge rather than impressions.
- **Adaptation is treated as the main obstacle.** Your nose stops reporting within minutes,
  so the course borrows a second nose at hour three and runs several lessons blind.

## On sourcing

Every mechanism claim is either tied to a source listed at the foot of its lesson, or
written as an inference and labelled as one. Where the research found folklore contradicted
by a study, the lesson says which is which: coffee beans do nothing between smells
(Grosofsky 2011); "Iso E Super adapts fastest" is contradicted by the one laboratory test
(Mignot 2021); and the brands' instruction to spray one perfume over another is contradicted
by the practitioners who tested same-spot against side-by-side. The default placement
throughout is therefore two zones sharing the air, and one lesson exists so you can check
that on your own skin rather than take it from here.

Note lists are marketing copy, not formulas. Anything drawn from them is a place to look,
not a result.

## Technical

A single self-contained HTML file — no build step, no dependencies, no tracking. Entries
you type persist in your own browser and never leave it.

Generated from a private source that tracks a specific collection, so the bottles named are
real ones. Published as a static mirror; the shelf data itself stays private.
